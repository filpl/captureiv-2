FIELD NOTES · RESEARCH

What the research says
about construction technology.

Short, practical reads on the methods behind project capture. Each note links to the original study and distinguishes research findings from day-to-day application.

01 / DOCUMENTATIONCASE STUDY

Why construction photos need a place and a date

An Auburn University study examined digital photo documentation through an industry interview, a hospital construction case and questionnaires. In the case, dated, indexed photos helped the owner revisit concealed building conditions during later decisions. It was one building project in the southeastern United States, so it offers a useful example rather than a universal measure of cost or performance.

Deloach, Liu & Kramer · CITC-VII, 2013Read the study ↗
02 / AERIALFIELD METHOD

Repeatable drone flights for progress monitoring

Jacob-Loyola and colleagues describe a workflow that pairs periodic UAV imagery and photogrammetric point clouds with a 4D BIM schedule. Their residential-building case study highlights the value of planned, repeatable capture when teams need to compare actual work with the plan. The workflow also depends on careful flight coverage and image processing; an aerial photo by itself is not a verified progress measurement.

Jacob-Loyola et al. · Sensors, 2021Read the study ↗
03 / REPORTINGVISUALIZATION

Putting planned and built progress in one view

Golparvar-Fard and co-authors proposed overlaying a 4D as-planned model on time-lapse site photographs. The method is designed to make planned-versus-observed progress easier to inspect and discuss. It relies on aligning the model view with the camera viewpoint, so good reporting still needs consistent capture and context—not just more images.

Golparvar-Fard et al. · Journal of Computing in Civil Engineering, 2009Read the study ↗
04 / WALKTHROUGHSYSTEMATIC REVIEW

Where virtual walkthroughs fit in AEC

A systematic review of 41 architecture, engineering and construction studies found virtual reality used for building inspection, facility management, safety training, education, and design review. That breadth suggests walkthroughs are most useful when spatial relationships are hard to communicate in flat plans. The review describes varied applications, not a guarantee that immersive tools improve every project decision.

Wen & Gheisari · Construction Innovation, 2020Read the review ↗
05 / SPATIALINFRASTRUCTURE CASE

Using terrestrial laser scans to track site conditions

A terrestrial laser-scanning study on Chicago’s Wacker Drive reconstruction used 23 scans across a 0.4-mile corridor to document construction, check bridge clearance and monitor progress. It shows how point clouds can preserve detailed spatial conditions for later comparison. Scan planning, registration and processing remain important parts of the method, especially when measurements need to be compared over time.

Zhang & Arditi · Infrastructures, 2020Read the study ↗
